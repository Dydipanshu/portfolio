Every semester, AKTU lets you *look* at your graded answer copies. It doesn't let you keep them. Looking meant about twenty minutes of paging through a slow portal, for each subject.

That's the kind of problem I like: small, annoying and shared by everyone.

## Start with the smallest thing that removes the pain

The first version wasn't an app. It was a script that logged in, walked the pages and saved images. It worked for me, and it was useless to anyone else, because nobody wants to run a script.

So the real work was taking everything the script knew and putting it behind **one button**:

1. Log in once.
2. Pick a course, then a subject.
3. Get a single PDF.

Each step hides something ugly: hidden form fields, cookies, dropdowns that change between semesters, images that sometimes fail to load. None of that is the user's problem.

## Don't hard-code what the portal can tell you

My first parser matched semester names exactly. Then the portal renamed one, and it broke.

The fix was to stop assuming. The app now reads the options from the live page and auto-detects the evaluation level. The portal can change its labels and the tool keeps working.

> If a value comes from a system you don't control, read it at runtime. Don't bake it in.

## Show honest progress

Stitching thirty images into a PDF takes a while. A spinner makes that feel broken. A counter reading **"18 / 30 pages"** makes it feel like it's working.

So downloads run as background jobs, and the client polls for real progress. It's a tiny change and it did more for trust than anything else I shipped.

## Make failures debuggable

Every error carries a code and a request ID. When someone messaged me "it didn't work", I could ask for one string and find exactly what happened.

## The result

Retrieval went from roughly **20 minutes to under a minute**, and more than 40 students have used it. What I'm proudest of is that the whole thing collapsed into a single action.

The lesson I keep coming back to: the technical part was the easy half. The work was in deleting every step between the user and what they wanted.
